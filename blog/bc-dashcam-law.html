B.C.'s New Dashcam Law: What Commercial Fleets Need to Know

Bill M217 will make British Columbia the first Canadian jurisdiction to require dashcams on heavy commercial vehicles. Here's what's confirmed, what's still pending, and how to prepare.

Compliance · British Columbia TrackingMe.ca Team · 8 min read

If you run commercial trucks in British Columbia — or send trucks through it, including U.S. carriers running the Alaska Highway — a new law is about to change what's required in the cab. Bill M217, the Dashboard Cameras in Commercial Vehicles Act, passed third reading in the B.C. Legislature on May 25, 2026, with all-party support, making B.C. the first province in Canada to mandate commercial dashcams.

Here's what fleets actually need to know, based on what's been publicly confirmed so far.

What the bill requires

As reported, Bill M217 requires a forward-facing dashcam on every commercial vehicle with a gross vehicle weight rating (or registered gross weight) exceeding 11,793 kg — roughly 26,000 lb. In practical terms, that's semis and heavy straight trucks. Light commercial vehicles, cargo vans, and pickup trucks fall outside the scope as currently reported.

Reported technical expectations for the camera include continuous recording at 1080p or higher, night vision, and retention of at least 72 hours of footage. Notably, driver-facing or in-cab cameras are not required by this specific bill — the mandate, as reported, is for forward-facing coverage only.

Because the rule is written around vehicle weight class and B.C. roads rather than where a truck is registered, it's expected to apply to any qualifying vehicle operating in B.C. — including out-of-province and U.S. carriers passing through.

When it takes effect

The bill passed third reading on May 25, 2026, and is expected to take effect approximately six months after royal assent — which, based on public reporting available at the time of writing, would put the likely compliance date in late 2026 or early 2027. The exact royal assent date and the final in-force deadline had not yet been published when this article was written.

What's still unconfirmed

A few important details remain to be finalized in regulation rather than the bill text itself:

  • The exact royal assent date and final compliance deadline
  • Complete published technical specifications (resolution, night vision, and retention requirements as legally binding regulation, not just reporting)
  • Enforcement mechanisms and penalties for non-compliance
  • Any exemptions by vehicle class, route, or operation type

Until B.C. publishes final regulations, treat the details above as the best available public information — not a substitute for the government's own published requirements.

How fleets should prepare

You don't need to wait for the final regulation to start getting ready:

  • Inventory your qualifying vehicles. Identify which trucks in your fleet exceed the 11,793 kg threshold and regularly operate in or through B.C.
  • Talk to your telematics provider about hardware readiness. Ask specifically whether a forward-facing camera option meets the resolution, night-vision, and retention specifications reported for this bill — and how quickly it could be installed fleet-wide once the deadline is confirmed.
  • Don't over-commit to unverified specs. Since final regulations weren't published at the time of writing, avoid locking into a hardware contract based solely on secondhand reporting — confirm requirements against the government's own published regulation before your compliance deadline.
  • Watch for the in-force date. Set a reminder to check for royal assent and the resulting six-month countdown, since that determines your real deadline.
Legal disclaimer: This article reflects publicly reported details about British Columbia's dashcam legislation for heavy commercial vehicles as of the time of writing and is provided for general awareness only — it is not legal advice. Final hardware configuration, technical specifications and compliance deadlines depend on the applicable vehicle type, operation, and the finalized provincial regulations. Confirm current requirements directly with the Government of British Columbia or a qualified legal/compliance advisor before making purchasing or compliance decisions.
